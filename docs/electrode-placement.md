# Electrode Placement

Good electrode contact matters more than most beginners expect. A poor contact can make a good circuit look broken.

## First Forearm Test

Use the forearm because it is easy to contract and relax on command.

1. Put two measurement electrodes along the target muscle direction.
2. Keep the two measurement electrodes close enough to observe the same muscle group.
3. Put the reference electrode on a nearby low-activity area if your module uses a third electrode.
4. Keep electrode leads short and stable.
5. Avoid pulling or moving the leads during the test.

## Wet Electrodes

Wet gel electrodes are usually the easiest way to get the first visible waveform.

- They reduce skin-electrode contact impedance.
- They are more forgiving during first tests.
- They are useful for debugging whether the electronics chain works.

## Dry Electrodes

Dry electrodes are convenient, but contact pressure and skin condition matter a lot.

- Use consistent pressure.
- Do not press electrodes by hand during measurement.
- Strap or fixture the electrodes if possible.
- Slightly moist skin usually improves contact.

## Environment

- Move away from desktop computers, chargers, power adapters, and monitors if the waveform is dominated by 50/60 Hz noise.
- Battery power is preferred for first tests.
- Keep input wires short and symmetric.
